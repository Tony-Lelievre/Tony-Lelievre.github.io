      <h2 id="irp-overview">A France–Chicago collaboration</h2>
      <p class="irp-lead">This IRP brings together applied mathematicians, molecular simulation specialists and machine-learning researchers in France and at the University of Chicago.</p>
      <p>The project develops mathematically grounded methods for complex molecular systems, with a particular focus on high-performance computing and neural networks. It builds on long-standing collaborations between the French and Chicago teams and supports exchanges across mathematics, biophysics, chemistry and scientific computing.</p>

  <section class="irp-section" aria-labelledby="irp-science">
    <h2 id="irp-science">Scientific programme</h2>
    <p>The project studies the mathematical and computational challenges raised by stochastic molecular dynamics, from equilibrium sampling to rare transitions and reduced models. Machine learning provides new tools, while mathematical analysis is essential to understand their accuracy, robustness and limitations.</p>

    <ul class="irp-axes">
      <li><strong>High-dimensional functions</strong>Neural representations for committor functions and other quantities central to molecular modelling.</li>
      <li><strong>Dimension reduction</strong>Collective variables, latent representations and effective dynamics for complex systems.</li>
      <li><strong>Generative models</strong>Efficient sampling strategies combined with statistically exact acceptance and correction mechanisms.</li>
      <li><strong>Machine-learned force fields</strong>Fast, accurate potentials for molecular dynamics, supported by numerical analysis.</li>
      <li><strong>Metastability and rare events</strong>Mathematical models and algorithms for transition pathways, reaction times and transport.</li>
      <li><strong>High-performance computing</strong>Scalable methods that connect mathematical ideas with production molecular-simulation software.</li>
      <li><strong>Target applications</strong>Large collective motions in proteins and protein complexes, biological processes involving chemical reactions, RNA, intrinsically disordered proteins, pharmaceutical targets, biocatalysts and materials.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
